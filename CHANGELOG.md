## v1.13.11 (tag `v1.13.11`), 2026-11-20 (Sound v17 · Cadillac: gas cooling along the exhaust and flow loss at the pipe steps)

* **Sound v17 · Cadillac** is v16 (same sourced Walker shells, 3.07 m connector, 0.5 m tail and p9 damping) plus two generic physical effects. Nothing is fitted to the scorecard. It is picker-only, right after Sound v16 · Cadillac on Cadillac's one drop-down. p1 stays the default. There is no `?caddy=v17` or `?sound=v17` alias and no new URL. Generic parameters only; no Cadillac-only code.
* **Axial gas cooling (new generic set key `t_axial` = outside heat-transfer coefficient h_o).**
  * Each bank's exhaust run, from the head pipe to the tail (consecutive group-1/2 pipes), loses heat by the steady 1-D balance T_out = T_amb + (T_in − T_amb)·exp(−U·πd·L/(ṁ c_p)) (Incropera & DeWitt, *Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer*, ch. 8).
  * U = 1/(1/h_i + 1/h_o). Inside: Dittus–Boelter (1930), Nu = 0.023 Re^0.8 Pr^0.3 (laminar floor 3.66), Sutherland viscosity. Mass flow is the engine model's own exhaust flow per bank. Each pipe takes the gas temperature at its mid-length. The run starts at the model's existing head-pipe temperature.
  * h_o = 20 W/m²K for a car standing still: natural convection from a horizontal cylinder (Churchill & Chu 1975), 8–9 W/m²K on the 1¾ in pipe, plus radiation from oxidised steel (ε 0.79, Incropera Table A.11), 8–12 W/m²K at 420–500 K. Total 16–21 W/m²K. A moving car would see more (Hilpert cross-flow, roughly 50–80 W/m²K); 20 is the conservative still-air value.
  * Warm idle, model gas temperatures, v16 → v17: head pipe 616 → 594 K, muffler 616 → 488 K, connector 505 → 428 K, resonator 505 → 387 K, tail 505 → 380 K. At 2000 rpm and 0.4 throttle: muffler 761 → 713 K, connector, resonator and tail 617 → 681 / 651 / 641 K (v16's single tail temperature was below this run's cooling curve).
  * Effect: the 0.5 m tail pipe's half-wave and the two cans' pass bands (n·c/2L) no longer coincide, so the 210 / 470 / 930 Hz ring drops.
* **Flow loss at pipe steps (set key `port_bc`, generic block from the unreleased v16.1 work).** Every exhaust area step of at least 1.2 is a resistive junction carrying the linearised mean-flow loss Δp = K ρ U u (Borda–Carnot; Ronneberger 1967; Munjal, *Acoustics of Ducts and Mufflers*, 2014, ch. 2). Expansion K = (1 − A1/A2)², contraction K = 0.5 (1 − A2/A1) (Idelchik).
* **Cleaned scorecard (scorer v2, 183 windows, lower is closer):**
  * Combined (pooled + 0.25 × ring): v17 3.715, v16 4.191, p8 4.231. Segment bootstrap v17 − v16 −0.48 (95 % −0.59…−0.37).
  * Pooled: v17 2.880, v16 3.172, p8 3.063. By path, cabin / outside far / tail: v17 2.02 / 5.15 / 2.72; v16 2.09 / 5.82 / 3.70.
  * Ring: v17 3.34 dB, v16 4.08, p8 4.67.
  * Cabin tilt below 1 kHz, model − recording: v17 −2.18 dB/oct, v16 −2.21.
  * Recordings' own floor: pooled 2.37, combined 2.79.

* **Choosing the state while driving.**
  * Each can computes its live mean-flow Mach number from the engine model's live mass flow and gas temperature. It uses the same formula as `can_gas`.
  * The can uses the design state nearest in log Mach. The switch point is the geometric mean of the two Mach numbers, with ±20 % hysteresis.

    * switching state twice a second: 184.8 / 185.8 vs 186.9 / 187.4 (+1 %).
* **Generic fix: 1-D perforated tube (TMM rev 5).**
  * The old concentric-perforate cascade (rev 1–4) mixed left-to-right tube matrices with a right-to-left perforate link, then inverted the product. The effect was a negative-admittance perforate and waves running backwards in time.
    * On v14's cans, 85 % (muffler) and 100 % (resonator) of the S21 impulse-response energy fell at negative time.
    * Refined to fine segments, it did not converge to the textbook limit: 48.5 dB at 400 Hz for a 5 %-open tube.

    * It is solved as a nodal network, because a 4×4 matrix power overflows when the perforate is strongly coupled.
  * Checks:
    * It matches the independent 3-D plane-wave model within 0.02 dB from 100 to 1000 Hz (σ 0.2, L 0.3 m, d 1¾ in, D 0.13 m).
    * It converges with refinement.
    * 0 % of its impulse-response energy is at negative time.
  * The old path is kept unchanged for rev ≤ 4, so every older set stays bit-identical.

## v1.13.3 (tag `v1.13.3`), 2026-11-17 (Sound v13.4 · Cadillac: throttle lag fix)

* **Diagnosis.** The throttle lag on v13.x was not filter delay or CPU. On a throttle step 0.1→1 at fixed rpm, most of v13's rise came through the exhaust-temperature model: three lags in series (load 1.5 s → primary gas 0.4 s → tail gas 1.0 s, unsourced, from the v9.11 work-in-progress). * **Generic fix `gas_dyn` (parameter, off by default).** The exhaust gas temperature follows each engine cycle (Heywood 1988, ch. 6: exhaust gas temperature is set per cycle; slow EGT readings are thermocouple and wall lag). Port gas and load: one engine cycle (120/rpm s). Tail gas: plug-flow transport time = exhaust volume downstream of the ports (pipes + TMM can shells, 0.048 m³ for v13.4) / exhaust volume flow (η_v · MAP/P_atm · V_d · rpm/120 · T_prim/T_amb), bounded to one cycle … 2 s. Wall warm-up (25 s) is unchanged, so steady-state temperatures and levels are the same.
* **TMM checked, not changed.** Fitted S21 matches the frequency-domain TL within about 1–3 dB up to 3 kHz; S21 → 1 at low frequency; passive; `scalePassive` = 1. The remaining 2000 rpm gap to p2 is the TriFlow's real 400 Hz–4 kHz TL removing the steepened blowdown energy that carries p2's instant throttle cue. Not EQ'd or level-matched.

## v1.13.1 (tag `v1.13.1`), 2026-11-16 (Sound v13.1 · Cadillac: TMM physics fixes and a cheaper filter)

* **Diagnosis, not a retune.** v13 was about 23 dB too quiet: Walker 21158 mid-band TL was 44–54 dB (640 Hz 44.1, 1280 Hz 54.0) against a realistic 20–30 dB for a stock 1959 muffler. Textbook checks still held (expansion-chamber TL MAE 0.04 dB vs Munjal; Helmholtz notch 59.4 dB at 136 Hz). The cause was two generic TMM bugs, not the louvre-size or porosity estimates, and not FIR path-delay peel, impedance units or the sign of the Mach terms. A dHole/σ sweep on the broken concentric barely moved mid-band TL, because the waveguides had already collapsed.
* **Bug A — shadowed Mach in the perforated concentric.** `var M = m4id()` inside `tPerforatedConcentric` hid the mean-flow parameter (and, being hoisted, left `zPerforate` with `M === undefined`). The inner and annulus `tTube` calls then received a 4×4 as Mach; `1-M*M` was NaN; `cx(r, i || 0)` turned that NaN imaginary wavenumber into 0, so those ducts had no acoustic length. The Sullivan 4×4 quietly became perforate shunts between zero-length tubes — a lumped-cavity short. v13 keeps this legacy path so the released listening test stays bit-identical; the fix is opt-in (`rev >= 1`). `cx()` itself is not changed globally, because legacy v13 depends on the NaN-swallow.
* **Bug B — wrong S22 signs.** For the typ-0 convention (U both +x), `N22` must be `−A Z2 + B − Z1 C Z2 + Z1 D`. The code used the opposite Z1 terms, so even an identity matrix with equal areas gave `S22 = −1`, `r22+t12 ≈ 2`, and `scalePassive ≈ 0.71` (−3 dB on every Sij, including S21). After both fixes the 21158 can has scale = 1; 21159 still has a mild scale ≈ 0.917 (~0.75 dB) and that safety stays. Identity 2 in / 1.75 in area change is now P1=P2=1.

## v1.12.11 (tag `v1.12.11`), 2026-11-16 (CFL-correct waveguide length; Cadillac p3–p7 at 44.1 kHz)

* **Cause:** Cadillac p3–p7 (and any other set that uses a perforated muffler can) built 1 mm end-caps and ~4 mm perforate necks. At 44.1 or 48 kHz those lengths are a fraction of a travel sample once the tail gas is warm (`L/c·fs` ≈ 0.10 for a cap, ≈ 0.52 for a neck). The delay slot was clamped to 1 sample, leaving a stiff one-sample closed-end loop whose effective length jumped with sample rate and temperature. p1 and p2 use only long chambers (shortest pipe ≈ 9 samples) and were not affected. The owner heard the starter (same kernel, before the exhaust is hot and fully driven) then silence once the engine should run. Renders of p3–p7 at 44.1 and 48 kHz on this box (V8 / Chromium worklet, including crank → run) were finite and audible, so the blow-up was not reproduced here; the CFL violation was real and is the generic root cause to close. WebKit was not available to test JavaScriptCore on this box. CPU for p3–p7 is about 1.6× p1 (80–88 pipes vs 31) and can underrun a slow in-car CPU, but the fix is the physics length, not a mute or a quality downgrade.
* **Fix (generic, any car, any sample rate):** `buildNet` now takes the worklet `fs` and gives every waveguide a minimum length of one travel sample at a conservative hot exhaust speed (`√(γRT)` at 900 K) or at ambient for intake runners. Caps and necks therefore stay CFL-legal as the gas heats, at 44.1 kHz and at 48 kHz. A junction whose admittance sum is not a usable number is skipped, so a 0/0 cannot latch the network to NaN. Not a sample clamp, not Cadillac-only, no new URLs, defaults unchanged, one sound drop-down per car.
* **Identity:** Cadillac p1 and p2, the GT350, 911 p1 and the BRM are bit-identical to v1.12.10 at both 44.1 and 48 kHz. Cadillac p3–p7 keep their pipe counts and stay audible through crank → run at both rates, with a minimum delay of about 1.35 samples (warm) / 1.64 (cold), not a clamped 1. 911 sets that use a perforated can (p2, p4, p6, p7) get the same generic cap/neck floor (a small level change, about 1 dB on p2, 0.3 dB on p7).

## v1.11.43 (tag `v1.11.43`), 2026-11-15 (sound dropouts on iPhone: kernel reaches Safari's top JIT tier; 911 start-up spike removed)

* **Symptom (owner, iPhone / Safari):** the sound cut out while playing the 911 (p4 / p6), not with the GT350.
* **Cause 1, all v11 cars, worst for the 911 sets with side boxes:** in JavaScriptCore, the JavaScript engine in Safari, the per-sample kernel `kv8` was one function of about 38,000 bytecode units. It never left the middle compiler tier (DFG) for the top tier (FTL); bun / JSC reports this. The cost per 128-sample block, as a share of the 2.67 ms real-time budget, in a full worklet harness on this box (`deploy/engine-worklet.js`, game controls at 60 Hz, start, 4 pulls with gear changes, a view switch):

  | Engine | GT350 | 911 p1 | 911 p4 | 911 p6 | BRM |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | JSC | 61 % | 57 % | 81 % | 81 % | 3 % |
  | V8 / Chrome | 23 % | 16 % | 18 % | 18 % | 5 % |

  p4 / p6 have 42 waveguide pipes against 22 for p1 and 21 for the GT350, and the pipe loops ran about 10× slower in DFG. On a phone that meant no headroom.
* **Fix 1:** the kernel body is split into five helpers: arrivals, cylinders, exhaust nodes, intake and starter. The code is verbatim, in the same operation order. Every piece now reaches FTL. New mean / p99 per block:

  | Engine | GT350 | 911 p1 | 911 p4 | 911 p6 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | JSC | 22 % / 48 % | 17 % / 38 % | 19 % / 42 % | 19 % / 42 % |
  | V8 | 22 % | 15 % | 18 % | 18 % |

  The 911 p4 is now no more expensive than the GT350.
* **Cause 2, 911 only (`int_res`):** the 0.25 s silent intake pre-roll (`int_settle`) ran in one go inside the first audible block, at key-on, at a car or sound switch while running, and at cold start. That is about 12,000 samples of kernel work in one quantum. It measured 64 quanta of work in JSC and 15–19 quanta in V8: a guaranteed dropout.
* **Fix 2:** the pre-roll now runs one 256-sample chunk per block. The chunks, controls and order are the same, and the controller is held. The block outputs silence until the pre-roll is done, then the held block plays. Key-on worst block in JSC is 64 quanta of work before and 2.5 after. A sound switch holds its crossfade until the new source has finished its pre-roll, so there is no level dip.
* **Sound:** bit-identical to v1.11.42 for the GT350, the Cadillac and 911 p1–p6, rear and cabin, idle / pull / game start (54 renders, `test` harness in the work tree). For the 911 the identical output starts 46 blocks later (about 0.12 s at 48 kHz) after the key or a switch. The BRM is untouched.
* Not changed: `latencyHint` stays 'interactive' because a larger output buffer would delay throttle response; the per-block cost is fixed at the source instead. The worklet still posts a small `meter` / `eng` object every 32 / 8 blocks, the same for every car.
